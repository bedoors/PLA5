Evaluation of the students’ solution

The first step is right. The sphere doesn’t accelerate up or down, so the vertical component of the string’s force balances Earth’s pull: FS on Sp sin 60° = FE on Sp = mg. The mistakes come after that.

The whole string force is set equal to mv²/R

Their line: FS on Sp = mv²/R

The sphere’s acceleration, v²/R, points horizontally toward the axis, so mv²/R equals the net force on the sphere. Earth’s pull cancels the vertical part of the string’s force, which leaves only the horizontal component: FS on Sp cos 60° = mv²/R. At 60° that’s half of the string’s force, as the force diagram shows.

The radius is wrong

Their line: R = 0.36 m − 0.12 m

The sphere circles the rotation axis, so R is its distance from the axis: 0.360 m. The 12.0 cm only says where the string is tied. Subtracting it gives the sphere’s horizontal distance from the tie point, which isn’t the radius of any circle the sphere moves on.

sin 60° is entered as 0.5

Their line: the denominator under the square root

sin 60° = 0.866. The value 0.5 is cos 60°.

Corrected solution

VerticalFS on Sp sin 60° = mg

Toward the axisFS on Sp cos 60° = mv²/R

Divide the twotan 60° = gR/v²

Speedv = √(gR / tan 60°) = √((9.8 m/s²)(0.360 m) / 1.732) = 1.43 m/s

PeriodT = 2πR / v = 2π(0.360 m) / (1.43 m/s) = 1.58 s ≈ 1.6 s

The mass cancels, and neither the 50.0 cm string length nor the 12.0 cm offset is needed. As a check, the students’ 0.70 s would swing the sphere out until the string is only about 12° below horizontal, almost level with the rod.

Model: the sphere is a point mass on a light string that doesn’t stretch, with g = 9.80 m/s². Damping on the sphere’s motion relative to the rod makes any swinging die out, so the sphere settles into steady circular motion; once the motion is steady the damping does nothing. The photo’s labels are measurements and disagree by about 1 cm: 12.0 cm + 50.0 cm × cos 60° = 37.0 cm, not 36.0 cm. Because the period depends only on the radius and the angle, the sim keeps 36.0 cm, 60° and the 12.0 cm offset, which makes its string 48.0 cm long.
